# Chat scroll navigation — implementation notes

> Agent-facing reference for [`docs/features/chat-scroll-nav.md`](../../features/chat-scroll-nav.md). The human-facing surface lives in that file; the implementation details and source paths live here.

## Implementation

- Markup lives in `frontend/src/components/chat/Chat.jsx` (`nav.chat-view__scroll-nav`). The four buttons are `--top` (Go to top), `--prev`, `--next` and `.chat-view__jump` (Latest, `refs.jumpBtn`), so Latest keeps the old jump-to-bottom counter logic. `--top` calls `scrollTranscriptToTop(refs)` in `frontend/src/components/chat/scroll.js`, wired as `onJumpToTop` in `useChatState.js`.
- **The rail is a folded overlay anchored at 35% of the transcript's height.** `.chat-view__scroll-nav` is `position: absolute` in `.chat-view__transcript-box` with `right: env(safe-area-inset-right, 0px)`, `top: 35%` and `transform: translateY(-50%)`, so its centre sits on the anchor and the four slots grow evenly around it. An earlier revision anchored it to the bottom (`bottom: calc(24px + var(--nav-col) + var(--nav-gap))`) and a revision before that made it a `flex: 0 0 auto` row, which reserved ~24 px of reading height on every phone. Do not bring either back: `scripts/test-responsive-layout.mjs` asserts `position === 'absolute'`, that the rail's centre lands on 35 % of the transcript's height, and that the transcript still reaches the surface below it.
- **Folded / unfolded is one DOM attribute.** `data-expanded="false"` on the nav folds each button (`transform: translateY(var(--fold)) scale(0.2)`, glyph `opacity: 0`) into a dot whose `--fold` places it around the rail's middle; `"true"` restores the slots. The nav's own box, grid and anchor never change, so unfolding never moves the rows or the other buttons.
- **Unfold/fold is imperative, in `Chat.jsx`.** `onClickCapture` on the nav unfolds when `data-expanded` is `"false"` **and** `e.detail` is non-zero (a pointer tap). `detail === 0` is a keyboard-triggered click, so Tab + Enter/Space still reaches the button instead of being swallowed by the unfold; `onFocusIn` unfolds too, so a focused control is always visible. Every tap outside (a capturing `pointerdown` listener on the document, removed once it fires) folds it again.
- **The folded rail carries its own hit area.** Folding scales each button's `::before` down with it, so `[data-expanded="false"]::before` paints a 28 × 40 px strip at the rail's middle with `pointer-events: auto`; a tap on it lands on the nav and unfolds it. `test-responsive-layout.mjs` asserts the strip's width, height and `pointer-events`, and that a real pointer press at the rail's centre unfolds it without scrolling.
- **Pills stay centred in their slot (`justify-items: center`).** A revision hugged them to the slot's right edge (`justify-items: end`); because `::before` grows the hit area 4 px on every side and the rail's right edge is the chat's right edge, that pushed 1–2 px of hit area past the frame and made the whole chat scroll sideways by 1 px at 360–430 px. `test-responsive-layout.mjs` catches it (`assert.ok(layout.overflow <= 1)`).
- **The rail eyes no longer swallow <kbd>Space</kbd>.** The buttons are in the tab order and the transcript itself is focusable (`tabIndex: 0`, `aria-label="Chat transcript"`), so <kbd>Space</kbd> scrolls the focused transcript and activates a focused navigation button. The earlier revision kept the rail at `tabIndex: -1` because a focusable button swallowed Space; that is solved by focus, not by hiding the buttons from the keyboard. `test-responsive-layout.mjs` Tab-walks to `--prev`, presses Space on the transcript and asserts it scrolls, then focuses Latest, presses Space and asserts `data-mode` returns to `pinned` with focus back on the transcript.
- **Hiding focused Latest must not drop focus on `body`.** `updateJumpButton()` returns focus to the transcript with `{ preventScroll: true }` before hiding the button (covered by `scripts/test-chat-scroll-nav.mjs`).
- **Next stays rendered but disabled while pinned**, instead of being display-hidden: the message targets must not swap places. `updateJumpButton()` sets `nav.querySelector('.chat-view__nav-btn--next').disabled = pinned`.
- The painted control is 20 px with a transparent `::before` at `inset: -4px` filling its 28 px slot. The column used to be 44 px slots around 26 px pills; at that size it read as "way too big" and cost every row 36 px, so it was shrunk on request. Do not grow it back. Both `width`/`height` on the button are required, not just `min-*`: a flex item ignores a bare min-size without a definite size.
- **The rail sits flush with the chat's right edge, over the scrollbar lane** (`right: env(safe-area-inset-right, 0px)`, no `--nav-scrollbar` term in `right` any more). It covers only a short stretch of the bar at mid-height, so the bar stays draggable above and below it; `test-responsive-layout.mjs` asserts exactly that with `elementFromPoint` at the top and bottom of the transcript. `syncNavScrollbarLane(refs)` in `frontend/src/components/chat/scroll.js` still publishes the measured lane as `--nav-scrollbar` on `.chat-view__transcript-box` from the transcript's `ResizeObserver` callback, but no rule reads it any more: it is now only a measurement, and it (with `scripts/test-chat-scroll-performance.js`) is the leftover of the flush-right decision. Removing it is safe once nothing else wants the lane.
- **The rail reserves no strip.** The rows are the plain reading column (`width: 100%; max-width: --read-max; margin-inline: auto`). A `--nav-gutter` reservation used to shorten every row so the rail had a lane of its own; measured on a phone that cost each message 20 px of width. It was removed on request: *"Remove added Space — move the buttons to allow using the scroll bar."* `test-responsive-layout.mjs` asserts the rows still reach the reading column at every width — the assertion that fails by 20 px if the reservation comes back. This replaced the old `padding-right` reservation on top-level `.tool-card--progress`, which protected only that one card.
- **The rail is a one-column grid of `--nav-col` slots.** `grid-template-columns: var(--nav-col); grid-template-rows: repeat(4, var(--nav-col))` with a 2 px gap: Top (row 1), Prev (row 2), Next (row 3), Latest (row 4, kept allocated while hidden so the others never move). Latest's badge is `position: absolute` on the pill's top-right corner instead of widening the pill, so it stays inside the control's own 28 px column. The previous revision made the buttons themselves 50 px wide labelled pills (`3.5rem` columns): they covered a row of transcript text. Keep the slots equal to `--nav-col` so each hit area stays inside its own slot.
- **No visible text.** `aria-label` + `title` carry the meaning of each glyph; the only text any control renders is Latest's unread badge (`.chat-view__jump-count`). A label span moved the buttons under the user's finger and made the cluster read as a toolbar.
- `updateJumpButton(refs)` in `frontend/src/components/chat/scroll.js` changes the rail's `data-mode` (`pinned` / `free`) and its visibility directly on the DOM, so scrolling never re-renders Preact. `noteTranscriptScrollTop(refs, scrollTop)` reuses the `scrollTop` the scroll listener has already read to decide whether the transcript overflows. It adds no layout read. It rewrites Latest's counter, so a test that sets `99+` by hand must do it after the fold animation settles.
- `scrollToAdjacentMessage(refs, dir)` reads row positions only when an arrow is tapped. The rail sits outside the transcript, so the user-intent tracker cannot see the tap. The function therefore unpins on its own and cancels any pending re-pin, which stops the scroll listener from pulling the view back down. Off-screen rows use `content-visibility: auto` placeholders, so the function lines the row up again for up to three frames as the real layout arrives.
- `scrollTranscriptToTop(refs)` unpins, sets `pinnedToBottom.current = false`, updates Latest's state and sets `scrollTop = 0`. It is the Top button's whole implementation.
- `findAdjacentMessage(tops, line, dir)` is the pure lookup, covered by `scripts/test-chat-scroll-nav.mjs` (part of `npm run test:chat-view`).
- The rail lives in `.chat-view__transcript-box`, a positioned wrapper that takes the transcript's flex slot. The rail's `top` is a percentage of that box, so it follows the transcript as the composer, docks or header take more or less room.
- A single tap always moves the view by at least 48 px, which is the `isNearBottom()` pin threshold. Without that floor, "previous" at the bottom picked the row cut off a few pixels above the top edge. The tap then barely moved the view, landed back inside the pin band, and re-pinned straight away, so the arrow looked dead.
- "Next" re-pins when the next row can never reach the top, because the view is already scrolled as far as it goes. The rail then returns to its pinned state, rather than unpinning with nothing left to scroll.
- Transcript rows have `flex-shrink: 0`. The transcript is a height-bounded flex column, and an off-screen `content-visibility: auto` row has a min-content height of 0. Shrinkable rows therefore collapsed to nothing, which broke both the scrollbar and the row offsets the arrows jump to.
- `node scripts/chat-scroll-nav-fixture.mjs [port]` serves the real chat view with a long fake transcript and in-page API stubs. Open it at a phone viewport to check the arrows by hand.

### Layout constraints the rail depends on

The 35 % anchor, `translateY(-50%)`, the 48 px floor, `justify-items: center` and `flex-shrink: 0` are all layout contracts rather than styling choices: change any of them and an arrow either leaves the transcript, overflows the frame by a pixel, reads as dead, or jumps to the wrong row. The fixture above is the way to re-check them by hand after touching the transcript's box model.
